My story

A little more about me

I’ve never really been the kind of person who feels satisfied just sitting still and letting life happen around me.

I like building things, fixing problems, leading when someone needs to step up, and helping people even when nobody is watching. A lot of what I’ve done — from cybersecurity projects and robotics to JROTC, Scouting, and community service — comes from the same place: I want the things I spend my time on to actually mean something.

I’m still figuring out exactly where life is going to take me, and I don’t pretend to have everything figured out. I’ve made mistakes, doubted myself, pushed myself too hard, and had moments where I wondered if I was doing enough.

There have been times when something I poured months of work into broke, plans completely fell apart, or I looked at everything in front of me and genuinely didn’t know how I was going to fix it. I’ve had nights where I sat there exhausted, frustrated, and wondering whether all the effort I was putting in was actually getting me anywhere.

Those moments taught me something I don’t think success ever could.

Sometimes there isn’t going to be somebody standing behind you telling you exactly what to do next. Sometimes the plan fails. Sometimes you lose progress. Sometimes you give everything you have and it still isn’t enough the first time.

And then you have a choice.

You can let that moment become the reason you stopped, or you can get up the next morning and start rebuilding.

I’ve learned that I’m probably never going to feel completely ready for everything life puts in front of me. Leadership doesn’t mean having every answer. Strength doesn’t mean never struggling. And growth definitely doesn’t mean everything goes according to plan. Sometimes it just means being scared, tired, or unsure of yourself and deciding to keep going anyway.

The hardest thing I’ve had to learn is that nobody is coming to build the life I want for me. The people around me can support me, teach me, believe in me, and push me forward — but eventually I have to be the one who takes the next step.

Technology is a huge part of my life, but it isn’t really the computers, code, or hardware that keep me interested. It’s what I can build with them. I love taking an idea that doesn’t exist yet and turning it into something real — whether that means creating software, solving a problem, or building something that can help other people.

And some of the things I’m proudest of aren’t things that worked perfectly. They’re the things that broke, challenged me, frustrated me, and forced me to become better before I could make them work again.

I also wouldn’t be who I am without my parents.

My dad has always been there for me. He’s supported me, believed in what I wanted to do, and given me someone I could count on when I needed it most.

My mom has always been the person who pushed me toward the finish line, even during the times when I couldn’t see it myself. She’s challenged me to keep going, to expect more from myself, and to not give up just because something got difficult.

I probably don’t say it enough, but I’m incredibly thankful for both of them. A lot of the drive, resilience, and values I carry with me came from the way they raised me and the way they’ve continued to stand behind me.

I’m proud of what I’ve accomplished, but I don’t want my accomplishments to be the most interesting thing about me.

Titles change. Projects end. Awards eventually collect dust.

What matters to me is the kind of person I become while earning them — someone who can be depended on, someone who steps forward when things get difficult, someone who helps when there’s nothing to gain from it, and someone who never becomes so successful that they forget where they came from or who helped them get there.

More than anything, I want to live a life I can look back on and know I didn’t waste. I want to keep pushing myself, keep learning, keep leading, keep helping people, and leave things better than I found them.

I know I’m going to fail again. I know there are going to be moments where I question myself again.

That’s part of it.

I don’t know exactly who I’ll be years from now.

I just know I’m not done becoming him.
